                             PROJECT 2: IDENTIFYING THREATS AND VULNERABILITIES IN AN INFRASTRUCTURE

1. IDENTIFYING THE DIFFERENT DOMAINS IN MY PLACE OF RESIDENCE

-Physical Security Domain
-Network/Internet Domain
-Devices and Equipment Domain
-Electrical/Power Domain
-People/Occupants Domain
-Access Control Domain
-Information/Data Domain
-Physical Hazard Domain

2. IDENTIFYING ALL POSSIBLE THREATS THAT CAN HARM MY RESIDENCE AND THE PEOPLE IN IT
The possible threats to my residence include:
- Burglary and theft
- Unauthorized access
- Cyberattacks
- Malware and viruses
- Wi-Fi/network attacks
- Data theft
- Fire outbreak
- Electrical faults
- Power surges
- Generator-related accidents
- Cooking gas leakage or explosion
- Damage to electronic devices
- Water leakage or flooding
- Physical injury
- Natural disasters such as heavy rain and storms

3. IDENTIFYING THE VULNERABILITIES THAT EXIST IN MY RESIDENCE PER DOMAIN
Physical Security Domain
- Doors and gates can still be targeted by intruders.
- Windows can be damaged despite having burglar proof.
- CCTV and alarm systems depend on electricity and proper maintenance.
Network/Internet Domain
- Many devices are connected to the Wi-Fi network.
- A large number of connected devices increases the possible attack surface.
- The router/network could be targeted by unauthorized users or attackers.
Devices and Equipment Domain
- There are many phones and electronic devices in the house.
- Devices can be infected with malware.
- Lost or stolen devices could expose personal information.
Electrical/Power Domain
- Extension boxes can become overloaded.
- Generator and electrical equipment can cause fire or electrical accidents if not properly maintained.
- Power fluctuations can damage electronic devices.
People/Occupants Domain
- People can accidentally expose information or make security mistakes.
- The child in the house may accidentally damage equipment or interact with hazardous items.
Access Control Domain
- Fingerprint systems depend on the access-control equipment functioning properly.
- Unauthorized access could occur if the system is compromised.
Information/Data Domain
- Important information is stored both physically and digitally.
- Phones and laptops containing important files could be lost, stolen, or compromised.
Physical Hazard Domain
- Cooking gas can cause fire or explosion if there is a leak.
- Water tanks and water systems can cause water damage if they develop leaks.
4. CATEGORIZE THE VULNERABILITIES INTO ADMINISTRATIVE, TECHNICAL AND PHYSICAL PER DOMAIN
DOMAIN	ADMINISTRATIVE	TECHNICAL	PHYSICAL
Physical Security	Poor security procedures	CCTV/alarm failure	Doors, gates and windows
Network/Internet	Poor password management	Router/Wi-Fi attacks	Router and network equipment
Devices & Equipment	Poor device management	Malware and hacking	Theft or physical damage
Electrical/Power	Poor maintenance practices	Power surge/electrical faults	Generator, cables and extension boxes
People/Occupants	Lack of security awareness	Accidental exposure of information	Physical accidents
Access Control	Poor access management	Fingerprint system failure	Unauthorized physical access
Information/Data	Poor information handling	Malware/data theft	Theft or loss of devices/documents
Physical Hazards	Poor safety practices	Equipment failure	Gas, water and electrical hazards


5. IDENTIFY THE THREATS THAT CAN TAKE ADVANTAGE OF THE VULNERABILITIES AS RISKS
Physical Security Domain
- Threat: Burglary
- Vulnerability: Doors, gates and windows can potentially be attacked.
- Risk: Unauthorized persons may enter the house and steal property.
Network/Internet Domain
- Threat: Cyberattack
- Vulnerability: Many devices are connected to the network.
- Risk: Attackers could gain unauthorized access or steal information.
Devices & Equipment Domain
- Threat: Malware or theft
- Vulnerability: Many devices contain personal information.
- Risk: Personal data could be stolen or devices could be compromised.
Electrical/Power Domain
- Threat: Electrical fault or power surge
- Vulnerability: Use of extension boxes and multiple power sources.
- Risk: Fire, electrical damage or damage to equipment.
People/Occupants Domain
- Threat: Human error
- Vulnerability: People can accidentally make security mistakes.
- Risk: Information or property may be exposed.
Access Control Domain
- Threat: Unauthorized access
- Vulnerability: Dependence on fingerprint access systems.
- Risk: Someone could gain access if the system is compromised.
Information/Data Domain
- Threat: Data theft
- Vulnerability: Important information is stored on phones and laptops.
- Risk: Sensitive information could be lost or stolen.
Physical Hazard Domain
- Threat: Gas leakage/fire
- Vulnerability: Cooking gas is used in the house.
- Risk: Fire, explosion or injury.
6. CATEGORIZE THE RISKS INTO CRITICAL, MAJOR AND MINOR
CRITICAL RISKS
1. Cooking gas leakage or explosion
2. Fire caused by electrical faults
3. Unauthorized access/burglary
4. Major cyberattack resulting in loss of important information
MAJOR RISKS
1. Malware infection on phones or laptops
2. Theft or loss of electronic devices
3. Power surges damaging equipment
4. Compromise of the Wi-Fi network
5. Failure of the fingerprint access system
MINOR RISKS
1. Minor physical damage to electronic devices
2. Water leakage from the water tank
3. Accidental damage caused by occupants
4. Temporary loss of internet connection
7. MITIGATION MEASURES FOR CRITICAL AND MAJOR RISKS
Critical Risk: Gas Leakage or Explosion
Mitigation:
- Regularly check the gas cylinder and connections for leaks.
- Keep the cooking area properly ventilated.
- Turn off the gas supply when it is not being used.
- Keep flammable materials away from the gas area.
Critical Risk: Electrical Fire
Mitigation:
- Avoid overloading extension boxes.
- Regularly inspect electrical equipment and cables.
- Use appropriate electrical protection devices.
- Disconnect unnecessary appliances when not in use.
Critical Risk: Burglary/Unauthorized Access
Mitigation:
- Maintain the CCTV and alarm system.
- Regularly check the fingerprint locks.
- Keep gates, doors and windows properly secured.
- Review security recordings when necessary.
Critical Risk: Major Cyberattack/Data Loss
Mitigation:
- Use strong passwords.
- Keep phones and laptops updated.
- Install security software where necessary.
- Back up important files.
- Avoid downloading files from unknown sources.
Major Risk: Malware Infection
Mitigation:
- Keep devices and applications updated.
- Avoid suspicious links and downloads.
- Use trusted applications and security software.
Major Risk: Device Theft
Mitigation:
- Keep phones and laptops secured.
- Use screen locks and biometric protection.
- Back up important information.
- Enable device tracking where available.
Major Risk: Power Surge
Mitigation:
- Use surge protection.
- Avoid overloading extension boxes.
- Disconnect sensitive devices during severe power fluctuations.
Major Risk: Wi-Fi Compromise
Mitigation:
- Use a strong Wi-Fi password.
- Change the password when necessary.
- Keep the router firmware updated.
- Do not share the Wi-Fi password with unauthorized people.
Major Risk: Fingerprint System Failure
Mitigation:
- Regularly maintain the fingerprint system.
- Ensure the system has a reliable power supply.
- Have an alternative authorized access method in case of system failure.
